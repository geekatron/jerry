## L0: Verdict Up Front

Among the three external-standard schemes assigned to me, **D — date-based `ADR-YYYYMMDD-slug` (log4brains)** is the clear champion; E (global monotonic `ADR-NNNN`, MADR) is the scheme log4brains *fled* and reintroduces the exact merge-race failure mode the research documents Jerry already avoids by construction; F (scope+slug-no-number) is just B (domain-slug) with the sequence number dropped, and inherits B's collision exposure without D's structural fix. Having picked D, my honest conclusion is a **split verdict, not a clean win**: D beats E and F outright, and D beats A/C on the one dimension the user's CRUX names as decisive (promotion/citation continuity) — D achieves the *same* zero-ID-churn promotion property B/C claim, but does so *without* B's latent collision surface, because a date partition is structural (no shared registry to race on) where a slug-only partition is social discipline. But D does **not** clearly beat B on Jerry's own terms, because B already delivers most of D's promotion benefit (an inference, not a measured figure) *and* preserves subject-legibility in the ID itself, which D's calendar-stamp ID sacrifices (mitigated only by the slug that travels alongside it — see [Attack on B](#attack-on-b-domain-slug-everywhere) for why that mitigation actually helps D more than it helps B). The most honest framing: **D is the strongest external challenger, and it wins convincingly against the internal alternatives that keep scope baked into the identifier (A/C), but the external-vs-domain-slug (D-vs-B) contest is genuinely close, and I cannot in good conscience call it more than a marginal win for D** — see [Honest Weakest Point](#honest-weakest-point).

## Champion Selection: D beats E and F

Before making the case FOR an external scheme at all, I rule out two of my three assigned candidates so the steelman is spent on the strongest one.

**E (global monotonic `ADR-NNNN`, MADR) is eliminated first.** MADR's own specification "assume[s] there won't be more than 9,999 ADRs in one repository" and requires a single repo-wide counter (research evidence base, line 98, citing `adr.github.io/madr`). A single shared counter is *precisely* the mechanism log4brains diagnosed as the cause of "conflicts during git merge when two developers created new ADRs on their respective branches" (research evidence base, line 100, citing log4brains ADR `20201016-use-the-adr-slug-as-its-unique-id`). Recommending E to Jerry would mean recommending the one convention external practitioners have publicly documented abandoning for this exact reason. E fails on its own school's stated grounds.

**F (`ADR-{SCOPE}-{slug}`, no number) is eliminated second, on different grounds.** F keeps scope in the identifier (like A/C) while dropping the sequence number (like B without its NNN). It therefore inherits A/C's central weakness — origin is still baked into identity, so promotion still forces an ID change — while gaining none of D's structural collision defense (no date partition) and none of B's optional sequence-based disambiguation when two decisions in the same scope pick similar slugs. F is strictly dominated: everything F can do, either A/C or B already does with one dimension (number, or scope) intact instead of discarded. I found no repo evidence anyone has ever used F-shaped IDs (`grep` of the catalog in the research evidence base, lines 57-69, shows every family carries either a number or both a scope prefix and number — never scope+slug with nothing else).

**D (`ADR-YYYYMMDD-slug`, log4brains) survives as the sole viable external candidate**, and — critically — it is not merely "the least-bad of three options I was handed." It has a specific, verifiable feature neither of the discarded schemes has: log4brains adopted it *after* trying and rejecting a monotonic-number scheme in production, for the specific reason of merge-conflict avoidance (research evidence base, line 100). It is the only one of the three with a documented failure-driven design history rather than a first-principles guess. That is worth taking seriously before attacking the home-grown alternatives.

## Steelman Case for D

### 1. D structurally eliminates the collision-race that both E *and* B/F still carry — not just the one E was built to fix

Log4brains' documented rationale for `YYYYMMDD-slug` is merge-conflict avoidance from a **shared central counter** (research evidence base, line 100). The obvious objection is "Jerry doesn't use a shared counter — it already scopes by project ID (A/C) or would scope by domain-slug (B)." I directly verified this objection does *not* fully hold for B/F: the two-namespace recommendation in the research evidence base still assigns framework ADRs the ID `ADR-{domain-slug}-NNN` (evidence base, line 133) — a per-slug sequence. That "per-slug NNN" is *still a shared counter*, just partitioned by slug instead of by the whole repo. Two independent contributors picking the same or a near-synonymous domain-slug (e.g. two different projects both reaching for `persistence-strategy`, `routing-triggers`, or `output-path-resolution` — all three of which already exist as real domain-slugs in `docs/design/`, per the research evidence base catalog, line 59) would race for the same `NNN=001` under B/F with *zero structural safeguard*, only the "slug-uniqueness discipline" the user's own prompt flags as a requirement of B. I directly confirmed the repo has 66 local branches (`git branch -a`, verified this session) and at least 7 distinct committer identities in `git shortlog -sn --all` (verified this session: Adam Nowak 729, geekatron 439, Saucer Boy 393, saucer-boy 166, Malcolm 90, Kevin Horvatin 36, Tylere Zimmerman 15, plus bots) — real parallel-branch activity at a scale where slug reuse across independent branches is plausible. D's date component adds a **free, structural** partition on top of the slug: even if two branches pick the same slug on different days, there is no collision; even on the *same* day, the failure mode degrades gracefully to "two files, same date, distinguishable by slug" rather than a silent duplicate-number defect. D is the only one of the four schemes under evaluation (A/B/C/F) that removes the shared-counter dependency entirely rather than relocating it to a smaller (but still race-prone) partition.

### 2. D matches B's headline promotion benefit — zero ID churn — without B ever having been practiced in this repository

The CRUX names subject-encoded identity (B) as the strongest reason to break from origin-encoding, because Jerry's 3 real framework promotions all incurred an ID change under scope-encoded schemes (A/C). D shares B's key property: a date-stamped slug carries **no origin/scope information to invalidate on promotion** — `ADR-20260221-agent-definition-format` would be exactly as valid a framework ID as a project ID, because it never encoded "PROJ-007" in the first place. But I verified something the CRUX does not surface: **B, as actually practiced, does not exist anywhere in this repository.** Every one of the 11 catalogued project-scoped ADRs uses a scope prefix at birth (`ADR-PROJ010-NNN`, `ADR-PROJ022-NNN`, `ADR-PROJ031-NNN`, `ADR-EPIC002-NNN`, `ADR-STORY015-001`, `ADR-150-001` — research evidence base, lines 60-63); zero project ADRs were ever born with a bare domain-slug. The research's own two-namespace synthesis (C) *keeps* scope-prefixing at the project tier and only switches to domain-slug at the framework tier post-promotion (evidence base, lines 130-134) — meaning the standard the research actually recommends is not "B" at all, it is "A at birth, B after promotion," which still performs exactly one ID-format change per promoted ADR. D requires **no behavior change relative to what "true B-everywhere" would require**, and D has a closer, already-idiomatic analogue already running in this exact repository (see point 3) than "domain-slug from the first day of a project ADR's life," which has never once happened here.

### 3. D is not a foreign import — Jerry already runs this exact identifier DNA, sixteen times, for a structurally identical artifact class

I searched the live repo for date-stamped identifiers and found `{slug}-YYYYMMDD-NNN` used as the canonical orchestration workflow directory name **16 times** across at least 9 different projects (verified this session via `find`, e.g. `PROJ-007-agent-patterns/orchestration/agent-patterns-20260221-001`, `PROJ-014-negative-prompting-research/orchestration/neg-prompting-20260227-001`, `PROJ-031-cowork-skeleton/orchestration/adr-convention-20260702-001` — the very directory this file lives in). Orchestration workflow IDs are, like ADRs, artifacts that (a) need a globally-legible sequence, (b) are created by autonomous agents rather than typed by a careful human counting up from the last number, and (c) must never collide across parallel project work. Jerry's own framework already solved that exact problem with `slug-YYYYMMDD-NNN`, not with a project-ID-scoped monotonic counter. Adopting `ADR-YYYYMMDD-slug` (or its date-first ordering `ADR-{slug}-YYYYMMDD-NNN` to mirror the orchestration convention precisely) asks Jerry to extend a pattern its own agents already execute correctly and un-self-consciously, rather than to adopt something genuinely foreign. This is the single strongest piece of evidence that D is *not* an outsider's imposition — it is the external community's answer converging with Jerry's own unstated practice in a sibling artifact class.

### Supporting fact: Jerry already treats "date" as first-class ADR provenance metadata — D just moves it into the ID

Every ADR file I inspected already carries a `Created:` date (e.g., `ADR-PROJ031-001` → `Created: 2026-06-26`; `ADR-PROJ031-003` → `Created: 2026-06-28`; `ADR-output-path-resolution-001` → `Created: 2026-03-31` — all verified directly this session) and Jerry's own in-body amendment convention already uses dated blocks (`AMENDED 2026-07-02 (Phase 3...)`, verified in `ADR-PROJ031-001` this session). D does not introduce a new concept to track; it takes metadata Jerry already records by hand in the body and promotes it to the one place — the identifier — where it becomes collision-resistant and grep-able without opening the file.

## Honest Weakest Point

**The core motivating problem D solves may not be Jerry's actual problem.** Log4brains' rationale is merge-conflict avoidance among *multiple engineers independently claiming the same next sequence number on parallel branches, simultaneously*. I searched this repository's history for direct evidence of that failure mode actually occurring — `git log --all --grep="ADR" -i | grep -i conflict` (run this session) returned exactly one unrelated hit (a validator conflict in a PROJ-041 commit message, not an ADR numbering conflict) and zero evidence of an actual git merge conflict on an ADR file, ever. The collisions the research evidence base documents (`ADR-001` appearing in `docs/adrs/`, PROJ-014, and formerly PROJ-031, evidence base lines 74-79) are **semantic/discoverability collisions across different directories, not literal git merge conflicts** — different paths never conflict at the git-merge-mechanics level; the confusion is human ("which ADR-001 do you mean?"), not a build-breaking artifact of concurrent authorship. This matters because it means the failure mode D specifically defends against (a race on a shared counter, resolved catastrophically by git) has **zero direct evidence of having occurred in this repository**, whereas the scope-prefix schemes (A/C) already deliver Jerry's actually-observed need — collision-free, human-legible IDs — without needing a date at all. If Jerry's real risk profile is "confusable IDs across independently-numbered scopes" rather than "simultaneous same-second counter races," then D is solving a more dramatic version of the problem than the one Jerry has actually hit, and A/C's simpler scope-prefix fix may already be sufficient for the *observed* failure mode, making D's chief selling point a defense against a hypothetical rather than a documented incident.

There is a second, compounding honesty point: I could not confirm whether the repository's apparent multi-name committer list (`Adam Nowak`, `geekatron`, `Saucer Boy`, `saucer-boy` — verified counts this session) represents genuinely independent concurrent engineers or one operator working through multiple personas/agent identities in a largely serial (not simultaneous) workflow. **This is inference, not verified fact** — I did not cross-reference commit timestamps for temporal overlap across these identities. If the true operating model is single-operator-serial rather than multi-engineer-concurrent, the entire class of "two people race for the same number" failures — the thing that makes E clearly bad and gives D its edge over B/F — is far less likely to manifest in practice than log4brains' original OSS-team context, and the practical difference between D and B's residual collision exposure (point 1 of the steelman) shrinks toward negligible.

**Mitigation:** Even if the collision-avoidance motivation turns out to be more defensive-in-depth than urgently necessary, D's other two steelman legs (zero-churn promotion identical to B's best property, and the already-idiomatic `slug-YYYYMMDD-NNN` precedent running 16 times in this exact repo) do not depend on the collision argument at all — they hold regardless of Jerry's true concurrency profile. So the honest concession is narrower than "D's case collapses": it is "D's *first* and most log4brains-authentic argument is the weakest of the three, resting on a documented-elsewhere failure mode with no direct local incident to back it," while legs 2 and 3 stand on their own local, verified evidence. A prudent recommendation would present D's collision-avoidance benefit as *structural insurance against a growing-branch-count future* (66 branches today, verified this session, trending up) rather than as a fix for a fire that has already been observed burning.

## Attack on A/C (Project-Scoped Sequence)

**Attack 1 — A/C bakes in, as a permanent tax, the exact citation break the user's CRUX identifies as the live wound.** The research evidence base is explicit: all 3 baselined `docs/design/` framework ADRs were born as project-scoped decisions and changed identity on promotion (`ADR-agent-design-001` carries `PS-ID: PROJ-007 | ENTRY: e-004` in a comment, evidence base line 59 and directly verified in the file header this session: `<!-- PS-ID: PROJ-007 | ENTRY: e-004 -->`). That is a **100% historical promotion-ID-change rate** across every framework ADR Jerry has ever baselined. A/C does not merely tolerate this — the research's own recommended promotion process for A/C requires a five-step manual remediation every time it happens again: determine scope, create a new ID, remap frontmatter, add bidirectional `PROMOTED_FROM/PROMOTED_TO` links, and **re-point every citation in every rule file that referenced the old ID** (evidence base, lines 160-164). If Jerry's stated core thesis is that knowledge accrual from projects into the framework is the *point* of the framework rather than an edge case, A/C converts the framework's central value-delivery mechanism into a recurring, manual, citation-fragile chore.

**Attack 2 — "Project-scoped" is not actually one namespace; it is an uncontrolled, still-growing zoo of scope tiers, each a fresh promotion hop.** The catalog shows scope prefixes at the project level (`ADR-PROJ010-NNN`), the epic level (`ADR-EPIC002-NNN`), the story level (`ADR-STORY015-001`), and even the GitHub-issue level (`ADR-150-001`) — four distinct prefix grammars, all called "project-scoped, collision-free" in the same breath (evidence base, lines 60-63). A/C's promotion story only accounts for one hop (project → framework). But if a story-scoped ADR (`ADR-STORY015-001`) needs to become an epic-level decision before it's framework-worthy, that is a *second* ID-churn event the documented promotion process (evidence base, lines 156-165) does not even address — it only discusses "project → framework," not the intermediate entity tiers the catalog itself proves exist. A/C's collision-freedom is real, but its promotion story is incomplete for its own catalogued sub-scopes.

**Attack 3 — A/C's promotion mechanic has zero external precedent and is admitted synthesis, not adopted best practice.** The research evidence base states this plainly: "No external source I found prescribes the concrete mechanic of remapping an ADR's ID and back-linking the original when it is physically promoted up a scope level... the 'renumber-on-promotion + bidirectional promoted-from/promoted-to link' recipe in L2 is my synthesis" (evidence base, lines 113 and 158). Choosing A/C means committing Jerry to inventing and indefinitely maintaining bespoke tooling and process for a problem (identity-preserving promotion) that external ADR practice — including log4brains' entire design rationale — solves by never needing an ID change in the first place. A/C is the only evaluated option that requires Jerry to build net-new governance machinery rather than adopt an off-the-shelf answer.

## Attack on B (Domain-Slug Everywhere)

**Attack 1 — B relocates the shared-counter collision risk; it does not remove it.** As established in the steelman (leg 1), the research's own operationalization of "domain-slug identity" still appends a **per-slug monotonic `NNN`** (`ADR-{domain-slug}-NNN`, evidence base line 133). That is a shared, incrementing counter with no built-in temporal or namespace partition beyond "hope nobody else independently picks this slug." Three real domain slugs already in `docs/design/` — `agent-design`, `routing-triggers`, `output-path-resolution` (evidence base line 59, directly verified in the `docs/design/` directory listing this session) — are generic enough phrasings that a plausible future project ("routing" changes for a different subsystem, a second "output path" decision for a different pipeline) could independently reach for the same or a confusingly similar slug and collide on `NNN=001` with **no structural safeguard**, only manual grep-before-you-name discipline. D's date partition removes this risk category entirely; B (and F) do not.

**Attack 2 — "requires slug-uniqueness discipline" is the user's own framing of B, and it is an unenforced social convention, not a governance control.** Compare this to Jerry's own constitutional posture: HARD rules exist precisely because Jerry does not trust "discipline" alone to hold under agentic, multi-session, multi-branch operation (see the entire HARD/MEDIUM/SOFT tiering apparatus in `quality-enforcement.md`, and the L5 CI-lint enforcement the research recommends specifically *because* MEDIUM-tier conventions are not self-enforcing, evidence base lines 178-182). Recommending B without a **repo-wide slug registry or CI lint that checks slug uniqueness across the entire filesystem before allowing a new domain-slug ADR to be committed** — which the research does not currently propose building — asks Jerry to rely on exactly the kind of unenforced convention its own governance model treats with structural suspicion everywhere else.

**Attack 3 — "True B" (domain-slug from birth, project-level included) has never once been practiced in this repository, making it a bigger behavior-change ask than D.** I directly verified every one of the 11 catalogued project-scoped ADRs uses an origin-scope prefix at birth — none is born with a bare domain-slug (evidence base, lines 60-63; cross-checked against the live `docs/design/` and representative `projects/*/decisions/` listings this session). The two-namespace synthesis (C) that the research actually recommends is not "B everywhere" — it is scope-prefix-at-birth, domain-slug-after-promotion, which is a hybrid that still performs exactly one ID-format change per promoted ADR (see Attack 1 on A/C). If Jerry wants B's promised "promotion = pure file move, zero ID churn" property for real, every future project ADR would need to break with 100% of existing project-ADR practice starting today. D asks for less behavioral change: it extends an identifier grammar (`slug-YYYYMMDD-NNN`) Jerry's own orchestration tooling already uses in 16 verified instances (steelman leg 3) rather than asking every future project ADR author to abandon scope-prefixing they have used without exception to date.

## The Crux — Sensitivity Analysis Against Promotion-Frequency

The user's prompt explicitly asks me to sensitivity-analyze the winner against the promotion-frequency assumption: **if promotion is rare (the historical exception), does D's case weaken? If promotion is the point (the common case going forward), does it strengthen?**

**Scenario 1 — Promotion stays rare (3 events in the framework's history to date, per the verified catalog).** In this world, A/C's citation-break tax is paid only occasionally, and its cost is bounded. D's promotion-related advantage (zero ID churn) becomes proportionally less valuable — you are avoiding a manual chore that happens a few times a year, not a chore that happens weekly. In this scenario, D's case rests almost entirely on legs 1 (structural collision defense) and 3 (idiomatic fit with existing orchestration-ID practice), *not* on the promotion argument — and per the [Honest Weakest Point](#honest-weakest-point), leg 1's urgency is itself unproven by direct incident evidence. **Under low promotion frequency, D is a solid but not urgent improvement over A/C; the case for changing convention at all weakens for every scheme, external or internal, because there is less problem to solve.**

**Scenario 2 — Promotion is the point (the framework's stated thesis: knowledge accrual from projects into `docs/design/` is Jerry's central value proposition, not an edge case).** In this world, every future ADR that succeeds at doing what Jerry says ADRs are *for* will eventually migrate scope. A/C's citation-break tax is paid on every success, which inverts the cost-benefit entirely: the more effective the framework is at its own stated mission, the more expensive A/C becomes. This is precisely the scenario the user's CRUX names as plausible, and it is the scenario in which D's zero-churn property stops being "nice to have" and starts being the dominant design criterion. **Under high promotion frequency, D (and B) both clearly beat A/C, and the contest narrows to D-vs-B on collision-safety (D's structural edge, per Attack 1 on B) vs. subject-legibility-in-the-ID-alone (B's edge, conceded honestly below).**

**My honest position on which scenario is more likely:** I found no direct evidence in the research base or my own inspection that quantifies future promotion rate — this is unknowable without a stated roadmap for `docs/design/` growth, which I do not have visibility into (labeled as a gap, not fabricated). What I *can* say from verified evidence: the framework's own self-description in `CLAUDE.md` treats "Jerry" as accruing "knowledge, wisdom, experience" as its core identity statement, and the research evidence base independently observed a *live* promotion-adjacent event happening mid-session (PROJ-031's bare-ID files being migrated to scoped IDs during the very research that produced the evidence base, evidence base lines 79-80, "the strongest internal signal that the team is already converging on project-ID scoping" — though note this was a *renumbering-within-project-scope* event, not a project-to-framework promotion, so it is suggestive of churn-sensitivity in general rather than direct proof of high promotion frequency specifically). Given the framework is young (first framework ADR dated 2026-02-21, per verified file headers) and already shows 3 promotions in roughly 5 months of ADR history, a naive extrapolation suggests promotion is not rare relative to the framework's total ADR count (3 promoted out of 3 framework ADRs total = 100% promotion rate for everything that has ever reached framework status) — which, if anything, argues Scenario 2 is the more empirically-grounded reading of the *observed* data, even though I cannot rule out this is a small-sample artifact of an early-stage framework.
